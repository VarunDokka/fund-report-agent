## Finding 1 — the "exam-stress pillar wins" story is really a 1–2 post story, not a category story

`pillars.md` currently ranks exam-stress POV as beating internship content by 5–25x. That's true in aggregate, but it's driven almost entirely by two posts, not the category as a whole.

Excluding the two outliers (209,496-view and 116,899-view posts — see Finding 2), the *other 5* exam-stress posts in the 28-post ranked list score 288, 173, 153, 118, and 89 accounts engaged — sum 821, average **164/post**. The *other 8* internship posts (excluding the exam-stress/internship hybrid outlier) score 299, 293, 196, 184, 148, 109, 89, 85 — sum 1,403, average **175/post**.

**That's a near-tie.** An ordinary exam-stress post and an ordinary internship post perform about the same. The pillar-1-beats-pillar-2 framing in `pillars.md` is real at the account-total level, but it's really "one specific post plus its same-day sibling beat everything else on the account," not "this topic reliably outperforms that topic." That distinction changes what "post more exam-stress content" should actually mean — see Finding 2.

## Finding 2 — what actually separated the 209K and 8,180 posts from the other 5 in the same category

The two strongest exam-stress posts share two things the five weak ones don't:

1. **A specific, vivid, almost poetic image or behavior — not a generic statement of the topic.** "so u lowk just float into the stars" and "rewarding myself after one exam as if my next one isn't in 1 day" are precise, visualizable moments. The five weak ones are close to just *naming* the feeling: "locked in but come too far to crash out," "4 lectures deep, brain not braining," "night before exam, deciding what to sacrifice." Same emotional territory, far less specific imagery.
2. **Same-day posting, likely mid-exam-period (23 May).** Both of the account's two biggest posts of the entire quarter went up on the same date. That's either a strong coincidence or a real seasonal-relevance effect — see Finding 3.

This is the same mechanic already logged in `guru-tracker.md` as Nick Tarmo's core teaching — hyper-specific content that makes the exact target person feel personally read — but this is the first time it's been confirmed *within the account's own data*, not just cited as an outside tactic. The actionable version: the lever isn't "make more exam-stress videos," it's "make the hook as specific and image-driven as the 209K post, regardless of which proven pillar it's in." Several of the weak exam-stress posts and most of the internship posts read more like topic labels than specific moments — that's the fixable gap, not the topic choice.

## Finding 3 — the account's biggest pillar has a seasonality problem, and it's live right now

The account's two biggest wins both post-date to 23 May — UK exam season. **Today is 2026-08-16.** There is no live exam period right now for most students. Posting generic exam-stress content this month risks measuring against May's numbers without May's actual situational relevance behind it. `instagram_analysis_varun_dokka.md`'s own recommendation #1 already flagged this ("especially around exam periods, deadlines, and results days") — this audit confirms it with the actual date math, not just the general advice.

By contrast, the internship pillar is Varun's *live, current* reality right now (the PE internship is presumably still running through the summer) — it doesn't have the same seasonality gap. This matters directly for "what to post next" — see the recommendation below.

## Finding 4 — a genuinely surprising, low-confidence signal on follower conversion (small sample, flag accordingly)

Computing new-follows per 1,000 views on the 4 top-5 posts that have follow data:

| Post | Views | New follows | Follows / 1,000 views |
|---|---|---|---|
| "float into the stars" (exam-stress) | 209,496 | 13 | 0.062 |
| "bed rotting" (internship) | 116,899 | 7 | 0.060 |
| "rewarding myself" (exam-stress) | 69,074 | 6 | 0.087 |
| "3 albums" (music/niche) | 42,064 | 6 | **0.143** |

The account's single biggest post by raw views (209K) converts followers at *roughly the same or lower rate per viewer* as its smallest top-5 post. The "3 albums" post — the account's one niche/personal-taste post that cracked the top 5, and explicitly called out elsewhere as underperforming as a category — actually converts new followers at more than double the rate of the biggest viral post.

**Read this carefully, don't over-index on it.** The sample is 4 posts and 6–13 follows each — this is not statistically solid, and it directly contradicts the personal/niche-content guidance elsewhere in this project. But it's consistent with a real, plausible mechanism: a huge relatable-joke post pulls in a broad audience that connects with the *joke*, not necessarily the *person*, while a smaller, more specifically-personal post (music taste — an actual fact about Varun, not a universal feeling) pulls a smaller audience that connects with *him specifically*, and follows at a higher rate. That mechanism, if real, is exactly the argument for the mastery/identity pillar in `pillars.md` — it's the pillar built entirely from specifically-personal material. Logged as H10 in `experiments.md` for real testing once daily data exists — this finding does not justify a strategy change on its own.
