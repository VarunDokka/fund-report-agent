# Production Playbook — On-Screen Text, Hooks, Tonality, Filming

Reusable rules derived from the creator-format-breakdown.md research + applied to the first tailored script (Private Equity story). Use this before scripting/filming any new video.

---

## On-screen text density — match it to content type

- **Rolling word-by-word / constant captions** → use for explainer/teaching/listicle content (thepostprotocol, harpercarrollai style). Text IS the information delivery.
- **One static sentence/question pinned the whole video** → use for short 15-25 sec single-punchline videos only.
- **"Anchor + Punch" (sparse, high-impact)** → use for story/philosophy/narrative content (trevorgetsholistic, austingeorgas style, and your PE-story script). One text card at the open (the scroll-stopping claim), then NO text until each quotable/killer line gets its own synced text card that appears and disappears with it. Rest of the video is just your face — this keeps the story feeling like a story, not a slideshow, while still hitting screenshot-able lines with text for muted viewers.

Rule: text density should match content density. Dense teaching = dense text. Story = sparse text at the 2-3 highest-leverage moments only.

## The Triple Hook (first 1-3 seconds, every video)

Three different attention mechanisms firing at once so the video works muted, with sound, or on a half-second glance:
1. **Text hook** — on-screen claim, different wording than the spoken line (not redundant).
2. **Verbal hook** — the spoken opener.
3. **Visual hook** — a pattern interrupt in frame (punch-in close crop that cuts to your normal wide frame, unusual first image, movement) — visual *change* in the first 1-2 seconds is what actually stops a scrolling thumb, more than the words do.

## Tonality/pacing default

Restrained confidence, not hyped and not monotonous:
- Default delivery: flat, matter-of-fact, unhurried. "I don't need your approval" energy (Nick's "you can watch this or don't, I don't really care" register), not motivational-speaker enthusiasm.
- Build in 2 deliberate pace breaks per video on your killer/quotable lines: slow down, drop voice slightly, then leave a real silent beat after the line before continuing. The pause sells the line — same delivery speed throughout makes punch lines disappear into the rest.
- No smiling/performed enthusiasm on philosophy/story content — flatness reads as credibility (fact, not pitch).

## Filming setup — phone front camera is sufficient, proven method (this is literally how Nick films)

1. Face the window (key light in front, never behind you). Overcast/golden hour ideal; diffuse harsh direct sun with a sheer curtain.
2. Stand 3-6 ft back from the window — too close blows out highlights.
3. Tripod/stand required — no handheld for this format. Locked-off frame is the visual language of sit-down philosophy content.
4. Framing: chest-up or shoulders-up, eyes in upper third, centered or rule-of-thirds off-center.
5. Eye-line: teleprompter/script must sit as close to the lens as physically possible (tape script directly under/around the camera) — off-axis reading breaks direct-to-camera intimacy.
6. Audio: phone mic echoes in bare rooms — film near soft surfaces (rug/curtains/couch) or use a lav mic if available. Audio quality affects retention more than video polish.
7. Background: personal but uncluttered — a shelf, plant, framed art. Not a blank wall (sterile), not busy (distracts).
8. Two-take structure for the punch-in open: film the full wide locked-off take first, then re-film just the first 2 lines closer/zoomed for the punch-in, cut close-up in for first ~3 sec then cut to wide for the rest. (Or fake it with a slow digital zoom on one wide take in CapCut.)

## CTA rule

Never end on "Follow" — it's a low-conversion, generic ask. End on a comment-bait CTA tied to a specific value exchange ("Comment 'X' and I'll send you Y") — comments are algorithmically weighted harder than follows and build a DM list. This is Nick's own signature move across nearly every transcript.

## Script content rule (applies to the 30-idea title bank too)

Titles/hooks without a real personal story/proof behind them are just vibes — they read as poster quotes, not teaching. Before scripting any "how to develop/build X" idea, confirm there's a specific, provable personal story attached. If there isn't, either find one or deprioritize the idea.
